# Plattegrond Studio — marketing brief

For the agency running our campaigns. Everything here is drawn from the working product, not from a
roadmap. Written in English because the repo is; the audience is Dutch, so the campaign copy itself
almost certainly wants to be Dutch — ask us and we'll supply the translated lines.

Working title: **Plattegrond Studio**. The name is not locked.

---

## In one line

Paste a Funda listing URL and get the home's real, editable floor plan in seconds — move the
furniture, knock a wall through, and generate a photorealistic render of the result.

## In one paragraph

Every listing on Funda with an interactive floor plan is sitting on real architectural geometry:
walls with thickness, doors and windows as actual openings, named rooms with their surface areas.
Today that geometry is locked inside a viewer — you can look at it, and that's all. Plattegrond
Studio pulls it out and hands it to you as a live drawing you can rearrange while someone is
watching. Then it turns the result into a photorealistic image, because the plan it renders from is
the real one, so the render doesn't invent rooms that aren't there.

---

## The problem, in the words of the people who have it

- *"I can see the plan, but I can't tell whether our sofa fits."* Buyers measure with their hands in
  the air, or not at all.
- *"They asked me during the viewing if the wall could go, and I said 'probably'."* Agents answer
  layout questions from memory, standing in a hallway.
- *"Styling costs money before we've even got the listing."* Sellers and stylists pay for
  visualisation up front, per room, per revision.

Every one of these is the same missing step: nobody can *change* the plan and see what happens.

## How it works — three steps, and that's the whole demo

1. **Paste the listing URL.** The real plan appears — every floor, every room named and measured.
2. **Change it.** Drag furniture in from a tray of 120+ pieces at real-world dimensions. Draw a wall.
   Click a wall and press *+ Door*. Rename a room and watch the m² update.
3. **Render it.** One button turns the plan into a photorealistic image — top-down, standing eye
   level, isometric "dollhouse", or watercolour sketch.

Under a minute, start to finish. This sequence is the ad, the demo video, and the landing-page hero.

## What makes it different (and hard to copy)

**It's the actual plan, not a picture of one.** The competition traces bitmaps or asks you to
redraw the home from scratch. We extract genuine vector geometry — on our reference listing that's
5 floors, 183 walls, 53 openings, 31 named rooms. Every measurement on screen is a real
measurement, and the m² of a room updates when you move its walls.

**The render knows what it's rendering.** The image generator isn't handed a drawing and left to
guess. It gets a written description built from the geometry itself: the footprint, every named
room with its area and where it sits, the furniture actually placed in each one with real
dimensions, which sides the windows are on so the light falls the right way, and explicit
instructions not to invent walls. That's why the output looks like *this* home rather than a
stock interior. It's also the part a competitor can't clone by adding an AI button.

**Nothing to install, nothing to learn.** It runs in the browser. The default mode has no tool
palette and no modes at all — one rule: *everything you add comes out of the ⊕ tray; everything
already on the plan you just drag.* Click any object and its controls appear on the object itself.
A pro mode with numeric inputs, layers and marquee select is one click away for people who want it.

**Your work follows you.** Sign in with an email address — a six-digit code, no password to
forget, leak or reset. Plans and renders sync across machines. Every edit is written locally first,
so nothing you do waits on the network.

## Feature inventory, in plain language

- **Import from a listing URL** — every floor, walls, doors, windows, room names and areas.
- **120+ furniture and fixture pieces** at real dimensions, across Living, Dining, Bedroom,
  Kitchen, Bathroom, Structure, Decoration and Garden.
- **Walls, doors, windows** you can add, move, resize and delete. Click a wall, press *+ Door*.
- **Text notes, arrows and measuring rulers** for annotating during a conversation.
- **Snapping, undo/redo, arrow-key nudging** — it behaves like a drawing tool should.
- **A description field on every object and room** — "wide oak floorboards, low winter light" —
  which feeds straight into the render.
- **Four render viewpoints**, scopeable to a single room, with your own style direction.
- **Lock the seed** to change one thing at a time, or re-roll for a fresh variation. Every render
  is kept, with a note of which render it came from.
- **Export** the floor as a PNG with a title block, or the whole project as a file.
- **Works offline** once loaded, apart from the initial listing import.

## Messaging angles worth testing

1. **"Does it fit?"** — the sofa-in-the-living-room question. Concrete, universal, instantly
   demonstrable in a five-second clip.
2. **"Ask the plan, not the agent."** — repositions the tool as the thing that ends guesswork in
   viewings. Strong for agents, slightly cheeky for buyers.
3. **"Before you bid, move in."** — emotional, aimed at buyers at the exact moment they're anxious
   and spending the most.
4. **"The wall goes here now."** — for the renovation and design crowd; leads with the editing,
   not the import.

Our instinct is that angle 1 sells the product and angle 3 sells the brand. Test both.

## Constraints — please don't write around these

- **Funda is somebody else's trademark.** We import from public listing pages; there is no
  partnership, endorsement or affiliation. Copy must never imply one. Use the name descriptively
  ("works with listings from Funda"), never as a badge, and never in a logo lock-up.
- **Not every listing has an interactive plan.** Where one is missing, the product falls back to
  pasting the page source or dropping in a floor-plan image and calibrating the scale by hand.
  Campaign copy should say "listings with a floor plan", not "any listing".
- **The listing URL passes through a third-party reader service** on import. If a campaign makes
  privacy claims, route the wording past us first.
- **AI renders are illustrative, not architectural drawings.** Never present one as a survey, a
  building plan, or a promise of what a renovation will look like.
- **Two people editing the same plan at once is last-write-wins.** Don't sell it as a collaboration
  tool.
- **The product name isn't final**, so avoid deep name-based creative (domains, wordplay, mascots)
  until it is.
