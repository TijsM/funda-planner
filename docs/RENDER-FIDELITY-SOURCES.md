# Render fidelity research — verified source notes

Raw per-angle output from the 2026-08-18 research sweep. Six angles, each swept by one agent and
re-checked by an adversarial verifier that opened the primary sources. Kept because the synthesis
in `RENDER-FIDELITY-RESEARCH.md` compresses it heavily.

## structural-control-papers

**What this angle says to do.** The sweep's central thesis survives verification and is now backed by BFL's own words: FLUX.2 has no structural-control channel, and its docs explicitly say structure is interpreted semantically rather than matched pixel-for-pixel. So no amount of prompt work fixes the drift — the conditioning channel has to change. Sequence the work by cost of being wrong: first spend one afternoon on the free changes (stop baking object names into the conditioning PNG, crop to drawing bounds, thicken wall lines) and stand up the ControlNet++ cycle-consistency metric — re-extract edges from each render and score F1/IoU per room — so every later decision is measured rather than eyeballed. Then run a three-way A/B on the same plans: today's FLUX.2 [max] at $0.07/MP, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 via fal-ai/qwen-image-edit-plus fed a vector-derived depth or lineart map (Apache-2.0, hosted, no ops, roughly $0.02/MP), and fal Z-Image Turbo ControlNet at $0.0065/MP with a true conditioning_scale. Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 is the option the sweep under-ranked and the one I would bet on for a commercial product, because it is the only path that is simultaneously licence-clean, hosted, and structure-aware; the FLUX.1-dev-derived ControlNets on fal and WaveSpeed all carry a non-commercial licence that no provider page grants you an exception to, and that question needs an answer in writing before any of them ships. Split the views into two pipelines rather than one prompt template — the top-down orthographic case is pixel-aligned re-texturing that a lineart control map at high strength can nearly nail, while eye-level and isometric need geometry the 2D map does not contain, which is the (weeks-long) argument for extruding the plan to grey massing and rendering depth/normal/segmentation buffers server-side. Treat "exact" as aspirational: 3DIS-FLUX's verified 58.9% instance success at six objects says layout control alone will not place thirty catalogue items perfectly, so the honest goal is a large, measurable reduction in drift plus a regression number that catches it when it returns.

### arXiv 2507.15724 'A Practical Investigation of Spatially-Controlled Image Generation with Transformers' — PARTLY-CONFIRMED

- **Gives us:** Paper is real: Xia, Hanspal, Tudosiu, Zhang, Parisot; submitted 2025-07-21, revised 2025-11-04. It 'establish[es] control token prefilling as a simple, general and performant baseline' and shows 'extending classifier-free guidance to control, as well as softmax truncation, have a strong impact on control-generation consistency'. The actionable recommendation is verbatim in the text: setting all guidance scales equal 'may lead to excessive degradation in generation quality, demonstrating the importance of separately tuning γy (CFG) and γc (ctrl-G)'. The anti-quantisation warning is verbatim: 'Quantising using the VQVAE hurts control consistency for VAR, without much effect on generation quality.'
- **Cost / effort:** Free. The takeaway — expose control strength and prompt guidance as two independent server-side knobs, tuned per view type — is a config change, not an architecture change.
- **Notes:** The specific digits in the sweep did not reproduce. Two independent reads of the tables returned mutually contradictory values (one pass: ctrl-G 1.0 → F1 39.3 / FID 5.69, ctrl-G 1.5 → F1 37.3 / FID 6.10; the other: ctrl-G 1.0 → 33.0, ctrl-G 1.5 → 35.9 on VAR-d16, and 30.8 → 37.1 on SiT-XL/2). The sweep's '36.9 → 39.9, FID 5.12 → 5.40' and its '6.2s vs 3.4s' adapter latency figures matched neither read. Do not quote any of these numbers; the directional claim (control guidance buys consistency at a quality cost, prompt CFG trades the other way) is supported by the abstract and is what matters operationally.
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15724, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15724v2

### ControlNet++ (arXiv 2404.07987) — headline gains and the evaluation protocol — PARTLY-CONFIRMED

- **Gives us:** Abstract confirms the paper and its three headline numbers over ControlNet: +11.1% mIoU (segmentation), +13.4% SSIM (line-art), +7.6% RMSE (depth), achieved by 'explicitly optimizing pixel-level cycle consistency between generated images and conditional controls'. - **Cost / effort:** Free to adopt as a regression metric: run an edge detector or wall segmenter on each render, score F1/IoU per room against the map we painted. A day of work; turns every option below into a measurable A/B.

### arXiv 2510.21763 — perspective prior, LineArt init, and the multi-ControlNet artefact ceiling — CONFIRMED

- **Gives us:** All three load-bearing claims verified verbatim. Training setup: 'For each control task, a dedicated ControlNet is trained while the pre-trained FLUX model remains frozen' and 'The modules are initialized from a pre-trained LineArt ControlNet to provide a foundational understanding of lines as structural guides.' Perspective prior: 'it consistently fails to render 3-point perspectives, often exhibiting "axis dropping" by ignoring vertical convergence'; 'the model shows a strong prior for straight horizons, likely inherited from both its training data and the base FLUX model.' Multi-ControlNet: 'Using guidance strengths optimal for a single module (e.g., 0.8) leads to image degradation... outputs suffer from severe color artifacts and "mushy" textures', and 'Stable generation requires reducing the guidance strength of each module to approximately 0.5.'
- **Cost / effort:** Free. Two directly actionable rules: stop trying to prompt the dollhouse tilt away (the prior is in the weights, it needs geometry); and if we stack depth + lineart, budget total control strength around 0.5 each rather than 0.8 each.
- **Notes:** Authors Julien Boudier and Hugo Caselles-Dupré, 2025-10-13. Confirmed: no quantitative metrics — 'both modules provide effective control but exhibit limitations with complex constraints'. Qualitative single-team work; treat the artefact threshold as a starting hypothesis to re-measure on our own content.
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21763, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21763v1

### The 3D massing / G-buffer route — right idea, wrong citations in two places — PARTLY-CONFIRMED

- **Gives us:** The recommendation itself is well supported — by Autodesk's depth/normal-map test and by 3DIS-FLUX's measured 62.9% ISR from a coarse depth map plus depth-controlled FLUX. Extruding our own wall/room/opening geometry to untextured grey massing and rendering depth, normal and per-instance segmentation buffers server-side is the only route to exact geometry in eye-level and isometric views, and it needs no photoreal renderer since we consume only the buffers.
- **Cost / effort:** Biggest engineering lift here: a headless three.js/WebGL offscreen or Blender worker in the render path, plus camera setup per view. Weeks, not days, and it adds a compute step before every generation. - **Notes:** Two citation corrections. arXiv 2503.15147 is 'PBR-Inspired Controllable Diffusion for Image Generation' (Xue, Guarnera, Zhao, Montazeri; 2025-03-18, rev 2026-02-07), a G-buffer-then-PBR-render pipeline — the sweep's quote about ControlNet 'substantially improving control over scene geometry and material attributes' is not in the abstract and I could not verify it. And Structured3D is released under a custom 'Structured3D Terms of Use' requiring a signed agreement form (code is MIT) — it is a research corpus, not something to train a commercial model on without permission. The sweep presented it as an available training/eval corpus.
- **Sources:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15147, https://github.com/bertjiazheng/Structured3D, https://aps.autodesk.com/blog/do-you-still-need-controlnet-testing-next-gen-models-viewer-scenes, https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05131v1
